MEERA'S VOICE & WRITING STYLE (Skinstinct)
==========================================

WHO SHE SOUNDS LIKE
A formulation scientist explaining things to a smart friend over coffee. Calm, exact,
not trying to impress. She'd rather you trust her METHOD than her brand. She states her
credentials and their limits up front ("I'm not a dermatologist. I don't have a medical degree.").

One-line summary: precise, openly admits what she doesn't know, cares about proof, and
sounds dry rather than excited.

------------------------------------------------------------
1. CORE MOVES (repeated constantly)
------------------------------------------------------------

a) "I'm not saying X. I'm saying Y."
   Main signature. Rule out the extreme reading before readers can misread her, then
   state the real, narrower claim.
   e.g. "I am not making a case that niacinamide doesn't work... What I'm saying is that
   the percentage on the label is the beginning of the question, not the answer."

b) Flags her own intent
   "I want to be careful here," "I want to be precise about what I'm not saying,"
   "The honest answer is," "I want to be honest about something."

c) Admits mistakes without being asked
   "it should have been right the first time," "it took us embarrassingly long,"
   "some of our testing is early stage." That openness is what makes people believe her.

d) Refuses to sell
   Says outright when she has no commercial stake: "I'm not selling a sunscreen... This is
   not a setup for a product launch." Skinstinct usually appears near the end, briefly,
   as EVIDENCE rather than a pitch.

e) Sends you to check, not to trust her
   Hands the reader a question to ask ANY brand:
   "You should ask for them. Not from us specifically - from any brand..."

f) "That's useful information."
   Closing line whenever a brand might fail your question: "If the answer is vague or
   doesn't arrive, that is also useful information." Never angry. Silence is data.

------------------------------------------------------------
2. STRUCTURE (how each piece is built)
------------------------------------------------------------
1. Hook: a concrete fact, number or scene. Never a question or a hype line.
   - "In 2021 I was sitting in a stability review meeting..."
   - "23% of our product returns came from..."
   - "The niacinamide serum you're using probably has..."
2. The gap: what the label or marketing says vs what's actually true.
3. Mechanism in layers: often "three things," explained one at a time in plain language
   (pH, then the delivery base, then batch consistency).
4. Fairness check: "I'm not saying..." and acknowledge the other side.
5. Her own practice: what Skinstinct does, stated modestly and often including a flaw.
6. Practical takeaway: what to ask for, or what to do.
7. Quiet close: no CTA hype. Often ends on a short, flat, understated line.

------------------------------------------------------------
3. SENTENCE-LEVEL FINGERPRINTS
------------------------------------------------------------
- Short line after a long one:
  "Most serums don't list their pH on the label. This is legal. It is also not helpful."
- "It is also..." pairing: state a neutral fact, then add a dry sting.
- Contrast reframes:
  "The base isn't decoration around the active. It determines whether the active arrives."
- Hard specifics: pH 3.2, 71%, 2mg/cm2, "about 14 months," "8 to 10 months."
  Exact figures, rarely round hype numbers.
- Dry understatement: "It is not a particularly inspiring story." /
  "She didn't, which is a reasonable outcome."
- British spelling: oxidise, sensitisation, colour, oestrogenic, standardised.
- Spaced hyphen as dash: "...the formula - pH, base, process - ..."
- All prose: no bullets, emojis, exclamation marks or hashtags.
- Calibrated certainty: "the evidence is solid and replicable," "the evidence base is
  thinner," "essentially absent." She grades how strong each claim is.

------------------------------------------------------------
4. VOCABULARY
------------------------------------------------------------
USES: documentation, CoA, stability testing, formulation, vehicle/base, concentration,
evidence, the gap, pH, batch, "worth investigating," "worth doing," "meaningful,"
"the honest thing."

AVOIDS (and sometimes mocks): glow, miracle, game-changer, "skin-loving," clean,
natural (except to take them apart), "clinically proven," any urgency/FOMO language.

------------------------------------------------------------
5. TONE RULES
------------------------------------------------------------
- Never villainises people: the people in the room weren't "incompetent or malicious...
  the system had a gap."
- Rejects binaries: natural vs synthetic is "one of the most unproductive framings in
  skincare."
- Respects the reader: assumes intelligence, explains the WHY, because "understanding the
  mechanism changes the behaviour more than repeating the instruction does."
- Warmth is brief and specific: "Thank you for reading these emails. I mean that
  specifically, not as a sign-off."

------------------------------------------------------------
6. LINKEDIN vs NEWSLETTER
------------------------------------------------------------
LINKEDIN
- Opening: straight into a fact, scene or date
- Stance: industry-facing, sometimes addresses other founders
  ("If you're building a skincare brand for India...")
- Close: a verification tip or a flat factual line

NEWSLETTER
- Opening: "Hi," then straight in
- Stance: personal, often starts from a customer's question
- Close: signs off "Meera", sometimes teases the next email
  ("More on specific actives in the next few emails.")
- Subject lines: plain and conversational, with a parenthetical aside
  e.g. "The Vitamin C email (I've been putting this off)"

------------------------------------------------------------
7. EXAMPLE: GENERIC vs MEERA
------------------------------------------------------------
GENERIC BRAND VOICE:
"Our hyaluronic acid serum deeply hydrates for plump, glowing skin! Dermatologist approved."

MEERA'S VOICE:
"Hyaluronic acid is listed on a lot of labels without its molecular weight. That omission
matters more than the percentage. High molecular weight HA sits on the surface and holds
water there. Low molecular weight HA penetrates further but has a thinner evidence base for
long-term use. I'm not saying one is better. I'm saying the label usually doesn't tell you
which one you're buying. If you ask the brand and they can't answer, that's useful
information."

MEMORY TRICK: "Precise, Pre-empt, Prove, Point."
Give the exact fact, rule out the misreading, show the evidence or your own flaw, then
point the reader to a question they can ask anyone.
